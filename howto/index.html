Violin Hero How to use

Play the violin part.
The piano plays the rest.

Violin Hero plays pieces for violin and piano while the score scrolls past. Listen first, then switch the violin off and play its part yourself: the app listens, draws your playing into the score and tells you how it went.

Get started

Five minutes from opening the app to your first scored take.

01 Tune up

Every start begins with a short tune-up. Allow the microphone, play your A string, then D, G and E. The line rises towards the middle as you get closer and turns green once the string is in tune.

Prefer to tune by ear? The tone key plays the string's pitch. Already in tune? My violin is already in tune skips ahead. While you tune, the app also sets the microphone's level for recording.

02 Pick a piece and play

Choose a piece in Repertoire by Level (Beginner to Expert), Genre and Piece. Press Play: the piano and violin play while the blue line follows the music. Drag the score to move around in it.

  • Play starts and pauses; Back and Forward move one bar (press twice for the start or the end).
  • Settings holds the Tempo, a Click to play with, the piano's sound, and switches for the Piano, its Staves and the Violin.
  • Input sets the microphone's level: aim for green while you play.
  • The panels fold away with their triangle and move by their handle, so you keep what you use at hand.

The first pieces are the starter pack's. More of them open as you practise, and even more come with content packs (see The Marketplace).

03 Record yourself

Put on wired headphones and press Record. The violin falls silent, a count-in clicks if the violin starts straight away, and the piano accompanies you. While you play, a line is drawn into the violin's staff, right where your pitch is:

  • in tune
  • a little off
  • clearly off
  • a note not heard

Press Record again to stop, or play to the end. Your take plays back with the piano straight away, and its line stays on the score. Every take is listed under Interpretation, so you can listen to it again later.

Wired headphones, please. Through speakers the microphone hears the piano as well, and Bluetooth headphones lag behind.

04 Your score

Play a piece from start to end and you get a score card: points for intonation, timing, notes missed and notes right, and, as the levels rise, for dynamics, trills and your sound. It says what went well and gives you one tip for next time.

Sign in (the round key at the top right) to keep your takes and progress on every device. A full take at 100 % tempo can then be published to the piece's top scores and the Hall of Fame. Everything else works without an account.

05 On a phone

On a phone the score fills the top of the screen and the controls sit in three tabs at the bottom: Transport, Settings and Repertoire. Swipe between them or tap a tab.

Turn the phone sideways for a wider score, with the controls in a column beside it.

Practising on your phone or tablet every day? Install Violin Hero as an app: it then opens from your home screen, without the browser around it.

06 Install it as an app

Violin Hero is a website: open violinhero.crescendu.com in a browser and it works, with nothing to download first. But it can also be installed like an app, free, without an app store:

  • ▣ Its own icon on your home screen or desktop
  • ⤢ The whole screen for the score, no address bar
  • ✈ Works offline: the scores and sounds are kept on the device
  • ↻ Always up to date: new versions arrive by themselves

iPhone and iPad

  1. Open Violin Hero in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button (on an iPhone at the bottom, on an iPad at the top right; in newer versions it may sit behind ⋯).
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.

Use Safari: in Chrome or another browser on an iPhone or iPad, open the page in Safari first (recent iOS versions also offer Add to Home Screen in Chrome's Share menu). Violin Hero reminds you with a little card at the top.

Android phones and tablets

  1. Open Violin Hero in Chrome.
  2. Tap the menu ⋮ at the top right.
  3. Tap Install app (or Add to Home screen), then Install.

In Samsung Internet: the menu ☰ at the bottom, then Add page to → Home screen. Chrome sometimes offers the install by itself at the bottom of the screen.

Windows, Mac or Chromebook

  1. Open Violin Hero in Chrome or Edge.
  2. Click the install icon at the right end of the address bar and then Install.

No icon? In Chrome: menu ⋮ → Cast, save and share → Install page as app; in Edge: menu … → Apps → Install this site as an app. In Safari on a Mac (macOS Sonoma or later): File → Add to Dock.

After installing, open Violin Hero from its icon. The first time it listens, it asks again for the microphone: allow it, and it remembers. Your account, takes and unlocked pieces are the same in the app and the browser once you are signed in.

Going further

The features that make practising feel like performing.

07 Real interpretations

Great violinists don't play like a metronome. For many pieces, Interpretation in Repertoire offers recordings by famous performers, from Fritz Kreisler to today's stars. Choose one and Violin Hero lines its own piano up with that performance, bar by bar: the piano breathes, slows and pushes on exactly where they did. Play and record with their phrasing instead of a strict tempo.

Press ▶ beside the list (or I) to hear the recording itself while the score follows it. Your own takes are in the same list, after the recordings.

08 Play along with YouTube

Some interpretations are YouTube videos. Press ▶ and the video opens in a small window while the score scrolls along in perfect sync, the blue line following the performer's every rubato. Drag the window anywhere; seek in the score or in the video and the other follows.

09 Vibrato, slides and trills

Violin Hero hears more than right and wrong notes. From the Intermediate level on, a little heart pops up over long notes just before they come: the place for a vibrato. When the app hears one, your line lights up pink and waves along with it, and the wider and steadier your vibrato, the bigger the celebration.

Slides A glide or a scoop into a note is drawn in pink. In Romantic and salon pieces a tasteful slide earns bonus points; in Classical and Baroque music it costs a few.
Trills A trill you play is drawn in purple, and the score card tells you how fast and how even it was.
Dynamics Play the p softly and the f out: from the Easy level on, the score card checks that your playing follows the markings.

10 Practise a passage

  • Marker: press it in the first bar of a tricky passage and again in its last. The passage is marked on the score and repeats until you switch Repeat off. Drag the marks' ends to change it.
  • Record a loop: with a passage marked, Record plays it round and round and records every pass, so you can hear yourself improve.
  • Tempo slows a piece down (the piano still sounds natural) until you have it, then bring it back up. Only takes at 100 % can go into the Hall of Fame.
  • Click adds a metronome on every beat, and Humanize lets the piano play like a person rather than a machine.

11 Make the score yours

Click a note to give it a fingering, a bowing, staccato, an accent or a tenuto; on one note also a dynamic or a fermata; select two (Shift-click, or a long press on touch) for a slur, a crescendo, a diminuendo or a rit. The violin plays your marks, and ↺ Edited takes them all off again.

  • Print: the Score key beside the piece makes a PDF of the violin part with your marks (signed in).
  • Share your markings: with no take chosen, Share sends a link to your marked-up score. A teacher's fingerings arrive as a version of the piece of their own.

12 Share and compete

Share a take

Share sends a link: whoever opens it hears your recording with the piano and sees your line on the score.

Hall of Fame

Publish a full take at 100 % from its score card to climb the piece's board and the Hall of Fame: top players, best intonation, best timing, most practice.

Practising now

Under the score you see how many other players are practising the same piece right now.

Invite a violinist

In the account menu: send your link to a friend. When they start practising, you earn karma, and the Hall of Fame lists the best inviters.

Your profile

Profile, in the account menu: your nickname, picture, city and a few words about you, with your practice this week, your pieces, highlights and achievements. Make it public and anyone can see it; the Hall of Fame then links your name to it.

13 The Marketplace

More music comes in content packs: collections of pieces with their scores, levels, genres and recordings to play along with, put together by teachers, arrangers and performers. You find them in the Marketplace, the bag key in the header. The Popular violin pieces pack brings 25 favourites, from the Méditation to Monti's Czardas.

Look inside

Explore opens a pack's page: every piece with its composer, level and genre, a glimpse of the violin part, its recordings and videos to listen to, and its top scores, each playable with the piano.

Add a pack

Sign in and press Add to Violin Hero. Its pieces appear in your Repertoire under their level and genre, all open at once, on every device you sign in on.

New versions

When a creator improves a pack, Violin Hero tells you what's new and asks: Update, keep your version, or decide later.

From your teacher

If your teacher uses Crescendu, packs made just for their students show up in a section of their own, seen only by you and your classmates.

Meet the creators

Click a creator's name to see who they are, what they teach or play, and all their packs.

Remove a pack

Under Your content packs, Remove takes a pack out of your Violin Hero again. The starter pack comes with Violin Hero and always stays.

Some packs may have a price: Buy opens a secure checkout, the invoice comes by email, and the pack is then yours like any other.

Want to share your own pieces? Teachers and musicians make packs on the Creator page, for everyone or just for their students: see the Creator's guide.

Also in the header: light or dark and five colour schemes (the sun key), English or German (the flag), and this manual (the ?).
