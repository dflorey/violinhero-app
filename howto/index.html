Operating manual FS-6

How to use Violin Hero

Pieces for violin and piano, played on a sampled grand piano and a solo violin, their score scrolling past on the display. Switch the violin off and play its part yourself, with the piano accompanying you.

01 Quick start

  1. Pick a piece at the bottom: step through the genres, the levels and the pieces there with the arrows either side of each readout.
  2. Press Play (or Space). Violin and piano play the piece; press it again to pause.
  3. Hear a real performance: the Interpretation window beside the piece steps through Default (as written) and its recordings. With a recording chosen, Play and Record play piano and violin to its timing; the lit ▶ in the window (or P) plays the recording itself, Piano and Violin switched off; switch either on to hear it along with the recording. The recording plays instead of piano and violin, from where the piece is, and the score follows it; press ■ to stop. A recording on YouTube plays as a video in a small window you can drag by its bar; the score follows the video, also when you play, pause or skip in the video itself.
  4. See the shape of a performance: under the score a graph shows the tempo as a text marker, higher where it is faster and thicker where it is louder: grey for the default interpretation (it follows the Humanize knob), green for the recording chosen. Its range in BPM is on the left. With a recording chosen, the piano also gets louder and softer as the recording does, keeping the accents written.
  5. Press Record (or R) to play the violin part yourself: two bars of clicks if the violin comes in straight away (none after a piano introduction), then the piano accompanies you, and what you play is drawn into the violin’s staff.
  6. Practise a passage: Marker at its first bar, Marker again at its last: it repeats.

Prefer a plainer look? The switch at the top right of the name strip, between Vintage and Modern, turns Violin Hero into a modern app filling the window: the same controls in the same places, as sliders with their values written out, named buttons and lists. Your piece, your place and your take stay as they are. It has a manual of its own.

02 The display

The display shows the whole score on one long line: the violin on top, the piano’s two staves below. While the piece plays, the score moves from right to left; the line in the left third of the display marks the moment you hear, and each note reaches it as it sounds. Above the score are the piece, its composer, the time played and, inverted, ↺ EDITED while you have annotated the piece, VIOLIN OFF while the violin is silent, ● REC while recording, REPEAT while a section repeats, NO MICROPHONE if it can’t listen, and LOADING SOUNDS or LOADING PIANO while instruments download. A section marked for repeating is shaded between two bracket lines, more strongly while it repeats; drag a bracket’s handle to move it, bar by bar.

Annotating the violin part

Click a note in the violin’s staff to select it: its head turns red. Shift- or Ctrl-click adds more (or takes one away). A toolbar opens beside the pointer (on a touch screen, along the foot of the display) and lights the marks the notes have, whether the score wrote them or you did: a fingering 0 (the open string) to 4, down-bow or up-bow, and staccato, accent or tenuto. Press one to set it; press one they all have to take it off, even one the score wrote. Revert gives the selected notes back their marks as written. Your marks are engraved just like the printed ones, and the violin plays them. With notes selected, the keys work too: 0–4, D, U, S, A, T, Delete to revert, and Esc to deselect. Annotations are kept for each piece. To go back to the score as written, click ↺ EDITED in the heading, then again when it asks REVERT?.

The playback follows what the score marks: dynamics and hairpins, accents such as sfz, tempo changes, fermatas, grace notes, trills and rolled chords (the wavy line before a chord: spread upwards from the beat). A “rit.” or “accel.” changes the tempo gradually, gliding over up to two bars, until “a tempo”. The violin plays as written: slurred notes join without a new stroke, each bowed note ends with its own recorded ending as the bow leaves the string, staccato is played spiccato, “pizz.” plucked until “arco”, tremolo bowed tremolo, separate strokes are a little apart, and the loudness of neighbouring notes only changes where the score says so. Repeats are played as written: repeat signs, first and second endings, D.C. and D.S. to Fine or the Coda; the score scrolls back to the repeated bars as they come round again.

03 Play, record, find your place

The row of keys under the blue plate:

  • Play plays from where it stopped; pressed while playing, it pauses there. From the very beginning with the violin off, it counts you in with two bars of clicks first (if the violin comes in during the first bar); if you have a take, it plays your take along instead. Double-click it to stop and go back to the beginning.
  • Back and Forward, the pair of keys beside Play: one bar back or on; double-click for the start or the end of the piece.
  • Input, left of Record, sets your microphone’s gain; the meter beside it shows its level from the first time you touch the knob or press Record (the browser asks for the microphone then), so you can set it before recording, and keeps showing it from then on: aim for green, the top yellow only on your loudest notes.
  • Record switches the violin off (you play it), counts in two bars with clicks (in the time signature and tempo where it starts), then plays from there and listens to you. Press it again (or let the piece end) and your take is played back straight away: the piano from where you started, with your recording where you played it. The take is kept: whenever the violin is off it plays along, wherever you go in the piece, until you clear it. Switching the violin on silences it; off again, it plays on where the piece is.
  • Clear, paired with Record, throws the take away, stops and goes back to the beginning, and switches the violin back on if it was on before you recorded. Its light turns green once a take is kept; press it twice (it blinks red in between) to clear.
  • Marker: the first press marks the start of the bar you are in, the second the end of the bar you are in then, and Repeat switches on; the third clears the section. Its light blinks while only the start is marked and stays lit once the section is complete.
  • Repeat repeats the marked section, seamlessly, for as long as it is on. Double-click it to jump to the section’s start.

You can also drag the score with the mouse or a finger: drag it left to wind on, right to wind back. While it plays, it pauses as you drag and plays on from where it stops. Flick it and it glides on, slowing down, as on a phone; touch it to stop.

04 The blue plate

Tempo slides from half to one and a half times the tempo written; the readout beside it shows the tempo playing at the playhead in beats per minute, following every change as it happens (a ritardando gliding down, a fermata, Humanize). Double-click the fader for the tempo as written. You can move it while the piece plays: it carries on from where it is. While you record, and while a take is kept, the fader is dimmed and doesn’t move: your take only fits the tempo you played it at. Clear the take to change the tempo again. The Humanize knob is locked the same way.

Click, the key with the metronome beside the readout, clicks every beat while the piece plays, with the count-in’s sound but softer, the first beat of each bar higher. Its light is green while it’s on.

Humanize turns the exact playback into a musical one, the further the more. It starts at 50 %; at 0 the piece plays exactly as written. Turned up, it follows rules from research on how musicians shape music: each phrase pushes on and grows towards its peak and relaxes at its end, the piece slows into its last bars, downbeats lean a little, the violin grows as it climbs, short notes are lighter, the piano’s melody sings out over its chords, long violin notes swell, and timing and loudness breathe a little. Where an arrangement marks no bowing, the violin slurs stepwise passages as a player would.

Reverb sets how much of the hall you hear, up to a large, long one; Volume how loud it all is. On a tablet or phone there is no Volume knob: use the device’s volume buttons.

Piano, a readout with arrows, chooses the piano (on narrow screens it is hidden and the grand plays): Grand (the Salamander grand), Upright 1 or Upright 2, two uprights recorded at three strengths each. A piano downloads the first time you choose it. Beside it, two keys side by side: Piano, the key with the grand piano, switches the piano part on and off; Show, the key with the staff, shows or hides the piano’s staves on the display: hidden, the violin’s staff alone fills it, much larger (the piano still plays); on small screens it starts hidden. The orange Violin key switches the violin part on and off.

05 Recording yourself

Record listens to your microphone (the browser asks the first time) and draws the pitch you play into the violin’s staff like a text marker, the notes showing through it, at the pitch you really played (in tune, on the note written; a semitone sharp of an E, on the F), and as thick as you played loud. Its colour shows your intonation: green in tune, through yellow to red at about a third of a semitone off. A vibrato shows as waves, drawn wider than played so you can see them, and is coloured by the pitch it swings around, so a good vibrato stays green. While you record, the head of the line glows, and long notes (over a second) earn a reward: fireworks when you hold them in tune, hearts and sweets for a nice vibrato. Out of tune, or not playing a note at all, you get skulls, poo, ghosts and thrown tomatoes, and those come quickly. Come in late and a snail or a sleepy face turns up; too early, a rabbit hops off ahead. Hold a note loud and long, and stars sprinkle over it. Playing your take back shows them all again. Where a note is due but nothing is heard, a wide light blue marker runs through it instead. Notes a little early or late still count. The line stays after you stop, until the next take; where the score repeats (repeat signs, endings, D.C., D.S.), each time round is kept apart: the repeated bars show only the line of the time round you are in, so going round again they are empty until you play them. A marked section going round is drawn afresh on each pass. It is smoothed, and short dropouts while you play are bridged, so it has no gaps. Your take starts a second before the violin’s first note, so a piano introduction isn’t recorded: ● REC on the display blinks until then and stays red while you are recorded. Violin Hero learns your setup’s delay from each take (how late your notes arrive) and moves the line back by it, so after the first take the line starts where the notes do.

Wear headphones while recording. The microphone hears everything in the room, so a piano coming from your speakers would be drawn into the line too. On an iPad or iPhone, Violin Hero listens with the device’s own microphone, not your Bluetooth headset’s, so the headset keeps its music quality. If it still sounds like a phone line, wired headphones or Bluetooth headphones without a microphone avoid it.
