Violin Hero How to use

How to use Violin Hero

Pieces for violin and piano, played on a sampled piano and a solo violin, their score scrolling past on screen. Switch the violin off and play its part yourself, with the piano accompanying you.

01 Quick start

  1. Pick a piece at the bottom: choose a Level and a Genre, then the Piece from the list (or step through them with [ ]).
  2. Press Play (or Space). Violin and piano play the piece; press it again to pause.
  3. Hear a real performance: the Interpretation list holds Default (as written) and the piece’s recordings. With a recording chosen, Play and Record play piano and violin to its timing; ▶ beside the list (or P) plays the recording itself, Piano and Violin switched off; switch either on to hear it along with the recording. The recording plays instead of piano and violin, from where the piece is, and the score follows it; press ■ to stop. A recording on YouTube plays as a video in a small window you can drag by its bar; the score follows the video, also when you play, pause or skip in the video itself.
  4. See the shape of a performance: under the score a graph shows the tempo as a text marker, higher where it is faster and thicker where it is louder: grey for the default interpretation (it follows the Humanize slider), green for the recording chosen. Its range in BPM is on the left. With a recording chosen, the piano also gets louder and softer as the recording does, keeping the accents written.
  5. Press Record (or R) to play the violin part yourself: two bars of clicks if the violin comes in straight away (none after a piano introduction), then the piano accompanies you, and what you play is drawn into the violin’s staff.
  6. Practise a passage: press Marker at its first bar and again at its last: it repeats.

Light or dark: Violin Hero follows your system, when it starts and whenever the system switches. The round key with the moon (or the sun), at the top right, turns it dark (or light) until then. In the dark, the score is light ink on dark paper.

Your account (optional): the round key with a person, at the far right after the ?, signs you in with your email and password or with Google, or creates an account. It is the same account as on Crescendu. Signed in, the key shows your profile picture, and every take you record is saved to your account (the score says Saving to your account, then ✓ Saved to your account). Your saved takes of a piece are listed in Interpretation after this session’s, marked saved: choose one to hear it again with its line on the score, its marks and its score. Without an account everything works the same: your takes are kept in this browser until you sign in, then saved to your account. Press the key to see who is signed in, to set the nickname shown in the top scores, and to sign out. The card with your score lists the piece’s top scores: the three best takes of all players.

02 The score

The large white card shows the whole score on one long line: the violin on top, the piano’s two staves below. While the piece plays, the score moves from right to left; the blue line in its left third marks the moment you hear, and each note reaches it as it sounds. Above the score are the piece, its composer, the time played, and small labels while something is going on: Violin off, ● Rec (red while you are being recorded), Repeat, No microphone, ↺ Edited once you have annotated the piece, and Loading… while sounds download. A section marked for repeating is shaded between two bracket lines, more strongly while it repeats; drag a bracket to move it, bar by bar.

Drag the score with the mouse or a finger to wind on (left) or back (right). While it plays, it pauses as you drag and plays on from where it stops. Flick it and it glides on, slowing down, as on a phone; touch it to stop.

Annotating the violin part

Click a note in the violin’s staff to select it: its head turns red. Shift- or Ctrl-click adds more (or takes one away). A toolbar opens beside the pointer (on a touch screen, along the foot of the score) and highlights the marks the notes have, whether the score wrote them or you did: a fingering 0 (the open string) to 4, down-bow or up-bow, and staccato, accent or tenuto. Press one to set it; press one they all have to take it off, even one the score wrote. Revert gives the selected notes back their marks as written. Your marks are engraved like the printed ones, and the violin plays them. Annotations are kept for each piece; to go back to the score as written, click ↺ Edited, then again when it asks Revert?.

The playback follows what the score marks: dynamics and hairpins, accents such as sfz, tempo changes, fermatas, grace notes, trills and rolled chords (the wavy line before a chord: spread upwards from the beat). A “rit.” or “accel.” changes the tempo gradually until “a tempo”. The violin plays as written: slurred notes join without a new stroke, staccato is played spiccato, “pizz.” plucked until “arco”, tremolo bowed tremolo. Repeats are played as written: repeat signs, first and second endings, D.C. and D.S. to Fine or the Coda.

03 Play, record, find your place

  • Play plays from where it stopped and becomes Pause while playing. From the very beginning with the violin off, it counts you in with two bars of clicks (if the violin comes in during the first bar); if you have a take, it plays your take along instead. Double-click it to stop and go back to the beginning.
  • Back and Forward (Navigation): one bar back or on; double-click for the start or the end of the piece.
  • Record (Take) switches the violin off (you play it), counts you in if the violin starts straight away, then plays from where you are and listens to you. It turns red and becomes Stop; ● Rec on the score blinks until your part begins. Press it again (or let the piece end) and your take is played back straight away: the piano from where you started, with your recording where you played it. The take is added to the Interpretation list as You with the date and time, and chosen: while it is chosen, whenever the violin is off it plays along, wherever you go in the piece. Each take you record is added beside the others; choose one again to bring back its tempo, its line on the score and the interpretation you played it to, and press ▶ beside it (or P) to hear it from where it began; Clear deletes it. Switching between interpretations while the piece plays doesn’t stop it: it plays on from where it is with the one you chose. They stay until you clear them or choose another piece (reloading the page loses them). Switching the violin on silences it; off again, it plays on where the piece is. With a stereo microphone (or a pair) the take is recorded in stereo; with one microphone it is mono, even on an interface that puts it on one side only.
  • Clear turns green while a take is chosen. Press it, then again while it asks Sure?, to throw that take away (your other takes stay); playing stops, you go back to the beginning, and the violin comes back on if it was on before you recorded.
  • Marker (Section): the first press marks the start of the bar you are in (it blinks), the second the end of the bar you are in then, and Repeat switches on; the third clears the section.
  • Repeat repeats the marked section, seamlessly, for as long as it is on, and nothing of the bar after it is heard. Repeat signs inside the section are played as written; one that goes back to before the section goes to its start instead, so it never leaves the section. Double-click it to jump to the section’s start.
  • A loop: with a section marked, Record switches Repeat on, counts you in and records the section round and round until you press it again. It is listed as Loop with the date and time; choosing it marks its section again, switches Repeat on and puts the playhead at the section’s start, ready to play back. While it is chosen, the markers stay as they are.
  • Your score: record the whole piece, from the very start to the end, and a card over the score tells you how it went: up to 100 points each for intonation, timing, sound (if the piece has long notes: vibrato on them; slides between notes, or into a note from a semitone or more below or above if they start before it is due and land on time (landing later counts as out of tune), drawn in pink on the line with a surfer or snowboarder riding them, earn bonus points in Romantic pieces, on top of the rest, and points off for bad taste in Classical and Baroque ones), dynamics (if the piece marks them: did you follow the p, f and hairpins?), trills (if the piece marks them: drawn in purple with a songbird or bee; how fast and how even), missed notes and wrong notes, added up to a total out of 100 times the categories scored. It is strict: a few clear misses count far more than many small slips, so 100 takes an excellent performance. The card adds stars, a word of praise and a tip for what to work on next. Click it to close it.
  • Share, beside Clear, lights up once you have a take and blinks while its score is shown. Sharing is coming soon.
  • Input sets your microphone’s gain. Its slider is also the level meter: your level lights its track from behind, green, then amber and red at the loudest, shining through the slider. It starts showing the first time you touch the slider or press Record (the browser asks for the microphone then) and keeps showing from then on, so you can set it before you record: aim for green, amber only on your loudest notes.

On a phone the buttons show their icons alone; hold or hover for their names.

04 Sound

Every slider shows its value beside its name; double-click a slider to put it back to where it started.

  • Tempo runs from half to one and a half times the tempo written; on the right, in blue, the tempo playing right now in beats per minute, following every change as it happens (a ritardando, a fermata, Humanize). You can move it while the piece plays. While you record, and while a take is chosen, it is locked (faded) until you choose Default or a recording, or clear the take: your take only fits the tempo you played it at. Humanize is locked for the same reason.
  • Click, the switch beside Tempo: a metronome on every beat while the piece plays, with the count-in’s sound but softer, the first beat of each bar higher.
  • Piano, a list: the Grand (the Salamander grand), Upright 1 or Upright 2, recorded at three strengths each. A piano downloads the first time you choose it. On phones the list is hidden and the grand plays.
  • Three switches, blue while on: Piano plays the piano part or not; Staves shows the piano’s staves on the score or hides them, so the violin’s staff alone fills it, much larger (the piano still plays; on small screens they start hidden); Violin plays the violin part or not.
  • Humanize turns the exact playback into a musical one, the further the more (50 % to start with, 0 % plays exactly as written): each phrase pushes on towards its peak and relaxes at its end, the piece slows into its last bars, downbeats lean a little, the violin grows as it climbs, the piano’s melody sings out over its chords, long violin notes swell, and timing and loudness breathe a little.
  • Reverb sets how much of the hall you hear; Volume how loud it all is. On a tablet or phone there is no Volume slider: use the device’s volume buttons.

05 Recording yourself

While you record, the pitch you play is drawn into the violin’s staff like a text marker, the notes showing through it: at the pitch you really played (in tune, on the note written; a semitone sharp of an E, on the F), as thick as you played loud. Its colour shows your intonation: green in tune, through yellow to red at about a third of a semitone off. A vibrato shows as waves, drawn wider than played so you can see them, and is coloured by the pitch it swings around, so a good vibrato stays green. Where a note is due but nothing is heard, a wide light blue marker runs through it instead.

The head of the line glows while you play, and you earn rewards: fireworks for long notes held in tune, hearts and sweets for a nice vibrato, stars for a note held loud and long. Out of tune or not playing at all, you get skulls, poo, ghosts and tomatoes, straight away; come in late and a snail or a sleepy face turns up, too early and a rabbit hops off ahead. Playing your take back shows them all again.

Your take starts a second before the violin’s first note, so a piano introduction isn’t recorded: ● Rec blinks until then and turns red while you are recorded. Violin Hero learns your setup’s delay from each take and moves the line back by it, so the line starts where the notes do.

Wear headphones while recording. The microphone hears everything in the room, so a piano coming from your speakers would be drawn into the line too. On an iPad or iPhone, Violin Hero listens with the device’s own microphone, not your Bluetooth headset’s, so the headset keeps its music quality. If it still sounds like a phone line, wired headphones or Bluetooth headphones without a microphone avoid it.
